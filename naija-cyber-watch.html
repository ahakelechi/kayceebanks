Emerging Trend

AI: the double-edged sword

Attack

  • Deepfake video beats KYC liveness checks at onboarding
  • AI-cloned voices impersonate bank staff — 3 sec of audio → 85% match
  • Generative AI builds synthetic identities from stolen data
  • Deepfake incidents in Africa up 7× between Q2 and Q4 2024

Defense

  • 87.5% of Nigerian fintechs now run AI for fraud detection
  • CBN mandates AI-powered transaction monitoring within 2 years
  • Target: cut fraud losses 70% by 2028
  • Reality check: reported false-negative rates as high as ~90%

Governance

  • National AI Strategy (NAIS) published Sept 2025 — 5-year plan
  • New National AI Trust: 10 experts + 2 ministers
  • NCAIR + research grants fund homegrown AI startups
  • Same generative tools, both sides of the fight

Regulatory Response

The Cybercrimes Amendment Act, 2024

72-hour reporting

Anyone who notices an attack or intrusion must report it to the National CERT within 72 hours

Two-year data retention

Service providers must retain traffic data and subscriber information for two years

Expanded surveillance

Security agencies may intercept communications without a court order in urgent situations

CBN cyber-framework deadline

Every regulated bank must submit an implementation roadmap by June 10, 2026 — most still lack a CISO to write one

Sources: TEMPLARS · Mondaq · Legit.ng · CBN circular (2024–2026)

Synthesis

Key takeaways

  • 01

    Attacks are more frequent, more automated (AI-assisted), and increasingly identity-focused.

  • 02

    Regulation now has real teeth — NDPC fines, 72-hour reporting, and CBN's June 2026 deadline mean compliance is no longer optional.

  • 03

    Critical infrastructure is a top target — a single vulnerability (like the CAC breach) can cascade nationally.

  • 04

    The talent shortage is the binding constraint — tooling and law can't outrun a lack of skilled defenders.

  • 05

    Zero-trust, identity-first defense, and cross-sector information sharing are the direction of travel.
